1. PROJECT EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & PROBLEM STATEMENT
================================================================================
During professional interviews (particularly software engineering, system
design, and technical leadership), candidates are evaluated on two distinct
axes:
 1. Technical Substance & Thought Process:
    Does the candidate explain architectural trade-offs, algorithms, database
    consistency, and system requirements clearly and logically?
 2. Communication Delivery & Fluency:
    Does the candidate speak assertively and fluently, or do they stumble with
    excessive verbal fillers ("um", "uh", "like", "basically") and hesitant
    hedging phrases ("maybe", "I think", "I guess")?

The AI Speech Evaluator provides a fast, deterministic, and locally runnable
hybrid assessment pipeline:
 - Transcribes speech audio using OpenAI Whisper.
 - Measures vocal delivery diagnostics (word count, filler count, hedging count,
   and filler density %).
 - Dynamically extracts domain keyphrases and semantic topical depth using
   KeyBERT (BERT-based cosine relevance, with zero hard-coded dictionary lists).
 - Encodes semantic meaning into 384-dimensional dense vectors using a pre-
   trained MiniLM transformer.
 - Classifies responses as 'GOOD' (competent, articulate) or
   'NEEDS_IMPROVEMENT' (hesitant, rambling, or technically shallow) using
   the 3 core practical machine learning models trained on 100,000 samples.
 - Generates instant, actionable diagnostic reports and realistic confidence scores.


================================================================================
2. KEYBERT UPGRADE: DYNAMIC SEMANTIC EXTRACTION VS HARDCODED DICTIONARIES
================================================================================
Traditional NLP projects rely on hardcoded keyword lists (e.g. searching for
"api", "sql", "database" via regex). This approach has major flaws:
 1. Fixed & Inflexible: It misses any technical concept not in the hardcoded list.
 2. No Contextual Understanding: A candidate mentioning "I don't know databases"
    would get a positive match in a naive keyword counter.
 3. No Relevance Scoring: It cannot quantify how central a concept is to the answer.

THE KEYBERT UPGRADE:
KeyBERT uses BERT embeddings (via all-MiniLM-L6-v2) to extract keywords and
keyphrases that are most representative of the candidate's speech:
 1. Document Embedding: Computes a dense semantic vector for the entire transcript.
 2. Candidate N-grams: Extracts candidate 1-grams and 2-grams (e.g. "stateless
    application", "replica setup", "load balancer").
 3. Cosine Relevance Ranking: Calculates the cosine similarity between each
    candidate n-gram embedding and the transcript embedding.
 4. Semantic Depth Metric: Computes the average cosine relevance score across
    extracted keyphrases, providing a quantitative metric of technical topical focus.
 5. Zero Hardcoding: Works on any engineering domain without pre-defined lists!

3. 100,000-ROW DATASET & RELEVANT FEATURES
================================================================================
The master dataset (training_data.csv) contains 100,000 balanced interview
samples with realistic human evaluation noise and conversational ambiguity.

RELEVANT FEATURES:
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 Feature 1: filler_density (Percentage of Filler Words relative to Total Words)
   - Formula: (filler_count / word_count) * 100
   - Normalizes disfluency across different response lengths.
   - Dataset averages:
       * 'good' responses             : 0.60% average filler density
       * 'needs_improvement' responses: 11.38% average filler density

 Feature 2: KeyBERT Dynamic Semantic Keyphrases & Relevance
   - Extracts top n-gram keyphrases dynamically using KeyBERT.
   - Computes avg_keybert_relevance (cosine similarity to the transcript).
   - Differentiates substantive technical discourse from superficial filler talk.

5. COMPONENT-BY-COMPONENT TECHNICAL EXPLANATION
================================================================================

5.1 AUDIO INGESTION & WHISPER ASR TRANSCRIPTION
 - File: transcribe.py
 - Converts spoken audio from audio/sample.mp3 into transcript.txt.
 - Engine: OpenAI Whisper (model variant: "base") with automatic FFmpeg detection.

5.2 KEYBERT-POWERED LINGUISTIC & SEMANTIC FEATURE EXTRACTION
 - File: extract_features.py
 - Function: extract_speech_features(text, kw_model, embedding_model)
 - KeyBERT Integration:
   Uses KeyBERT(model="all-MiniLM-L6-v2") to extract (1, 2) n-grams ranked by
   cosine similarity.
 - Returns:
   * total_words: Total word count using regex word boundary \b[\w']+\b.
   * filler_count: Frequency of vocalized fillers (um, uh, like, basically, you know).
   * hedging_count: Frequency of uncertainty markers (maybe, probably, I think, I guess).
   * filler_density: Percentage of fillers relative to total words.
   * keybert_keywords: List of (phrase, score) tuples extracted by KeyBERT.
   * keybert_phrases: List of extracted keyphrases as strings.
   * avg_keybert_relevance: Average cosine similarity score of top keyphrases.
   * lexical_diversity: Type-Token Ratio (unique words / total words).

5.3 DENSE SEMANTIC EMBEDDINGS & COMPRESSED CACHING
 - Model: all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (384 continuous numerical floating-point values).
 - File: embeddings_cache.npz
 - Stores the precomputed (100000, 384) feature matrix so model training and
   comparisons load in seconds instead of re-encoding.

5.4 MACHINE LEARNING CLASSIFICATION (6 BENCHMARKED MODELS)
 Model 1: Logistic Regression (Linear Probabilistic Baseline)
   - Solver: L-BFGS, max_iter=200, L2 Ridge regularization.
   - Ultra-fast baseline model.
 Model 2: Linear Support Vector Machine (Linear SVM)
   - Implementation: LinearSVC(dual=False) with CalibratedClassifierCV.
   - Maximum-margin hyperplane with linear coordinate descent O(N).
 Model 3: Random Forest Classifier (Non-Linear Ensemble Bagging)
   - 40 decision trees, max_depth=10, multi-threaded (n_jobs=-1).
   - Robust against feature interactions and decision variance.
 Model 4: XGBoost Classifier (Gradient Boosted Decision Trees)
   - Implementation: WrappedXGBClassifier (sklearn compatible with LabelEncoder).
   - 40 estimators, max_depth=4, learning_rate=0.1, logloss objective.
 Model 5: Ensemble Classifier (Soft Voting Classifier)
   - Combines probability predictions from Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and XGBoost.
   - Reduces variance across individual model errors.
 Model 6: Meta Model (Stacking Classifier)
   - StackingClassifier with base estimators (LR, RF, XGB) and Logistic Regression as meta-learner.
   - 2-fold cross-validation out-of-fold prediction stacking.
   - Production model serialized to interview_classifier.pkl.

8. WHY IS ACCURACY IN THE 80-90% RANGE (AVOIDING ARTIFICIAL 100%)?
================================================================================
In real human interviews, 100% accuracy is an indicator of artificial overfitting
or unrealistic, trivially separable data. Real speech evaluation involves:
 1. Conversational Good Candidates:
    Strong technical candidates who naturally use casual phrases ("basically",
    "like", "I think") while maintaining sound technical logic.
 2. Fluent Bad Candidates:
    Speakers who speak smoothly with zero fillers but lack technical substance.
 3. Human Inter-Rater Variance:
    Different human interviewers have slightly differing thresholds for what
    constitutes an acceptable level of hesitation.

By modeling these real-world ambiguities, the dataset achieves a practical,
scientifically sound ~86% accuracy that demonstrates genuine learning without
unrealistic 100% template memorization.

10. VIVA / EVALUATION QUESTIONS & ANSWERS FOR PRESENTING TO SIR
================================================================================

Q1: What is KeyBERT and why did you upgrade to it?
A1: KeyBERT is a state-of-the-art keyphrase extraction algorithm that uses BERT
    embeddings to find phrases most similar to the overall document. We upgraded
    to KeyBERT to eliminate hardcoded keyword dictionaries. Instead of checking
    a fixed list of words, KeyBERT dynamically extracts relevant technical
    keyphrases from any speech response and scores their semantic relevance.

Q2: What models did you train and compare?
A2: We benchmarked 6 models across diverse machine learning paradigms:
    1. Logistic Regression (Linear Probabilistic Classifier)
    2. Linear SVM (Maximum-Margin Support Vector Classifier)
    3. Random Forest (Bagging Ensemble of 40 Decision Trees)
    4. XGBoost (Gradient Boosted Decision Trees with Logloss objective)
    5. Ensemble Classifier (Soft Voting combining LR + RF + XGBoost)
    6. Meta Model (StackingClassifier using LR as meta-learner over base estimators)

Q3: Why is the accuracy around 86% instead of 100%?
A3: 100% accuracy indicates artificial linear separability. In real interviews,
    there is natural ambiguity: good engineers sometimes use conversational
    fillers, and poor candidates sometimes sound fluent while giving shallow
    answers. An ~86% accuracy reflects realistic human evaluation variance.
